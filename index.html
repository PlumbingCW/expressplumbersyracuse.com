Syracuse plumbing calendar

What usually goes wrong, and when

Winter: freeze riskHose bibs, garage lines and exposed pipes can split in a hard freeze. Syracuse City Code expects owners to protect the service line from frost.
Spring: irrigation startupSprinkler lines are charged again. Leaks show up as soggy strips and a sudden drop in pressure.
Summer: peak irrigationThe city plan lists a May to October secondary water season, and heavy use exposes weak lines and low pressure.
Fall: winterize and heatBlow out irrigation, drain outdoor lines and service the water heater before the heating load starts.

Plumbing emergency right now? Start here

Whatever has failed, the first minute matters most. These steps apply to nearly every emergency, and our 24/7 line will walk you through the rest.

Water is flowing where it should not

Shut the main water valve in the house, then call. Turn off the water heater if the water will be off for long. Do not touch the city meter valve, which belongs to the city.

Sewage is backing up

Stop using water and do not flush. Keep people and pets away from the affected room, and call the line. See emergency sewer line cleaning.

You smell gas

Leave the building first. Do not use switches or phones inside. From outside, call 911 and Enbridge Gas at 1-800-767-1689, then call us once the gas company says the area is safe.

Local plumbing

What Syracuse homes ask of their plumbing

Syracuse grew from 3,702 people in 1990 to nearly 40,000 today, so most homes are fairly new. That is good for pipe age, and it also means many homes share the same builder-grade fixtures and water heaters now reaching the end of their service life. A heater that is eight to twelve years old is worth watching.

Culinary water in Syracuse comes from city Well #3 plus water purchased from Weber Basin, and a separate pressurized secondary system waters yards. Keep the two apart. If a plumber, a landscaper or a handyman suggests tying an indoor fixture to irrigation water, say no and call us.

Sewer service runs through the North Davis Sewer District, whose treatment plant sits in Syracuse near the Great Salt Lake shoreline. If a backup affects only your house, the blockage is almost always in your own lines. If neighbors report the same trouble, tell the District as well.
