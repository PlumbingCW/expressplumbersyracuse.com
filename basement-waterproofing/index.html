Start with the source

Basement waterproofing in Syracuse, UT

Waterproofing starts with finding where the water comes from. A basement can take on water from the surface, from a high water table or from a plumbing leak, and each needs a different answer. We look at the pattern before proposing anything.

If water is rising now, see sump pump service and call the 24/7 line. This page covers the permanent fixes that follow.

Diagnosis

Four places basement water comes from

Surface runoff

Soil that slopes toward the house, or downspouts that dump at the foundation.

Wall seepage

Water pushing through cracks or the wall-to-floor joint after heavy rain or snowmelt.

Rising groundwater

Seepage from the floor that follows the seasons.

Plumbing leaks

A hidden supply or drain leak that looks like seepage. See leak detection.

Local note

Irrigation, grading and the wet basement

Syracuse yards are irrigated from May to October with pressurized secondary water, and overwatering beside a foundation is a common reason for a damp basement. Aim sprinklers away from the house and keep soil sloped down and away.

Syracuse City Code ties new home sprinklers into the secondary system and requires irrigation leaks to be repaired within 15 days of notice. A leaking sprinkler line beside the foundation is a repair to make before any waterproofing work.

Home checks

What you can check before anyone digs

  • Walk the foundation after a hard rain and look for pooling water.
  • Check that downspouts discharge several feet from the wall.
  • Look at window wells for debris, standing water and cracks.
  • Check the floor drain and the sump pit for blockage.
  • Note where and when the basement gets damp, and write it down.

That log is useful. A basement that is damp only after irrigation season points to the yard. One that is damp in spring points to snowmelt and groundwater. We use your notes to narrow the cause.

Be careful with claims

What waterproofing can and cannot promise

Drainage and pumps reduce water entry, and they depend on power, maintenance and the water load. A basement fix is not a promise against every storm. We tell you what a system is meant to handle and what it is not.

Foundation cracks that keep widening, walls that bow inward and floors that heave are structural signs. They call for a structural engineer, and we will say so honestly before selling any waterproofing.
