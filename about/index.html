  • Licensed and insured plumbers whose licenses you can verify with the state.
Check us

How to verify any Utah plumber

The Utah Division of Occupational and Professional Licensing runs a Verify a License tool at secure.utah.gov/llv/search/index.html. Utah plumbing license types include apprentice, journeyman, residential journeyman, master and residential master. Ask the plumber for the name on the license and check it before work starts.

Also ask your city what permits apply. Syracuse City Public Works is at 801.825.7235 and can answer permit questions for work on the service line.

What we will not do

Promises we do not make

We do not promise an arrival time, because traffic, weather and other emergencies change it. We tell you what we know when you call and update you if it changes. We do not claim a response window we cannot keep.

We do not quote a price over the phone for work we have not seen, and we do not push a bigger job than the problem needs. If a repair is the honest answer, we say repair. If a part is past saving, we say that too.

Local roots

Why Syracuse plumbing is its own thing

Syracuse is a young city. The Hooper Canal reached the area in 1884, the town board formed in 1935 and Syracuse became a third-class city in 1950. The population was 3,702 in 1990 and is now close to 40,000, so most homes here are newer than the plumbing in older cities.

That shapes the calls we get. Water heaters from the building boom are reaching the end of their service life, newer drain lines suffer more from wipes and grease than from corrosion, and irrigation season brings its own leaks. Knowing that helps us diagnose faster.

Sewer service runs through the North Davis Sewer District, formed in 1954, with a treatment plant in Syracuse. Water comes from city Well #3 and Weber Basin purchased water, and a separate secondary system serves yards. We keep those systems separate on every job.

Safety first

Gas, water and sewage each have their own rule

  • Gas smell: leave the building, then call 911 and Enbridge Gas at 1-800-767-1689 first. Do not use switches or phones inside.
  • Flooding: shut the main water valve, then call. Do not touch the city meter valve.
  • Sewage backup: stop using water, do not flush and keep everyone out of the affected room.

We repeat these on our service pages because they work. Knowing them before an emergency is worth more than any quote.
