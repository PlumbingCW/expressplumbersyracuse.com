What we install

Plumbing installation in Syracuse, UT

A good installation starts with the right shutoff, the right slope and the right vent, and ends with a pressure test that shows no leaks. We install fixtures, appliances and supply and drain lines in homes and small commercial spaces.

Local note

Pressure irrigation, and what you can and cannot tie into

Syracuse's secondary water is pressurized irrigation diverted from the Weber River, and Syracuse City Code ties new home sprinklers into it. That system is separate from the culinary water you drink. Never connect an indoor fixture to secondary water.

Any work that touches the irrigation system is reported to the city within 24 hours under Syracuse City Code. We keep the two systems apart, and we ask Syracuse City Public Works about reporting when your job touches either.

Process

How an installation goes

Scope

We confirm the fixture, the location and the supply and drain.

Prepare

We shut off water, protect finished surfaces and set the rough-in.

Install

We set the fixture, connect lines and seal.

Test

We run water, check for leaks and show you the shutoff.

Permits come first when the work calls for them, and we tell you before we start if one is needed.

After the install

What to check once the job is done

  • Run each fixture on hot and cold and look at every joint for weeping.
  • Check the shutoff valves turn freely and note where they are.
  • Look under the sink and behind the toilet again after a day.
  • Keep the manual, the model number and the installer's contact.

A slow drip that appears a day after an install is the most common callback. Check again at 24 hours and tell us right away if you see any moisture.

Common upgrades

Small additions that prevent big problems

  • A pressure regulator when household pressure is high.
  • New braided supply lines on every fixture you touch.
  • A shutoff valve for any fixture that lacks one.
  • A leak sensor under the water heater and the washer.

These are cheap next to the damage they prevent. We mention them during the scope, and you decide what to add.

Good to know

Why a licensed installer matters

A fixture installed wrong can leak inside a wall for months before anyone notices. A licensed plumber is trained to size, slope, vent and test the work, and manufacturers often tie warranties to a proper installation. Ask for the name on the license and check it with the state.

Code note

The standard behind a clean install

Utah's state construction code amends the International Plumbing Code (IPC) for use statewide, under Utah Code Title 15A, Chapter 3, Part 3. It covers things you cannot see after the walls close, such as drain slope, venting and trap arms.

A plumber working to the IPC does not guess at slope or vent distance. If a quote leaves out the permit or the inspection, ask why.
