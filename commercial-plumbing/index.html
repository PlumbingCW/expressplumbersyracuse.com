Overview

Commercial plumbing in Syracuse, UT

Emergency commercial plumbing in Syracuse, UT covers the calls that cannot wait for business hours: a floor drain overflowing in a kitchen, a leak in a ceiling over inventory, a backed-up restroom on opening day. The line is answered 24/7 for businesses and property managers.

We handle drains, leaks, fixtures, water heaters and the plumbing that keeps a small commercial building open. For large industrial systems or specialty process piping, we tell you honestly if the job is outside what we do.

After hours

Business emergencies: what to do before we arrive

  1. Shut off the water at the main for a leak, or at the fixture for a single failure.
  2. Stop using floor drains and restrooms if a line is backing up.
  3. Move stock and equipment away from the water, and turn off power to wet areas only if it is safe.
  4. Call the 24/7 line, and tell us whether the building is open to the public.
Kitchens and food service

Grease interceptors and floor drains

Restaurants lean on grease interceptors and floor drains, and both fail quietly. A full interceptor sends grease downstream and into the lateral. A floor drain with a partial blockage overflows at the worst time, usually during service.

We clear floor drains, check interceptor condition and recommend a pumping or cleaning schedule. For repeat grease clogs in the building line, hydro jetting cleans the pipe wall.

Water protection

Backflow assemblies and annual testing

A backflow assembly keeps water from flowing backward into the public supply. Syracuse City Code calls for backflow devices to be tested annually by a state-certified technician. Ask Syracuse City Public Works what paperwork the city wants after a test.

We can repair or replace a failed assembly and point you to certified testing. Do not let the annual test date slip.

Leaks and tenants

Leaks, property managers and tenant calls

Leaks in commercial space are hard to trace. Water travels along beams and appears far from the source. We trace it section by section and write up what we found for your insurer or landlord. See leak detection for how that works.

Property managers can set up one call for a building with several tenants. We note which units and fixtures belong to which tenant so the work orders stay clear.

Local note

Business in Syracuse: who to call first

The Utah History Encyclopedia names R.C. Willey as the largest business in Syracuse, and big retail and storage buildings sit alongside the town's homes. Large buildings have long drain runs, so a drain trouble often sits in the building line and not at a single sink.

For backups tied to District lines, the North Davis Sewer District lists emergency numbers on its page. For water service and meters, Syracuse City Public Works is the contact.

Code note

Backflow, grease and the standard

Utah's state construction code amends the International Plumbing Code (IPC) for use statewide, under Utah Code Title 15A, Chapter 3, Part 3. Commercial buildings add backflow assemblies, grease interceptors and larger fixture counts, each with its own requirements.

Property managers should keep a record of backflow tests and interceptor service. If the city or an insurer asks, you can hand it over.
