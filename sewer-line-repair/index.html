Repair or just clean

Is the sewer line broken, or only blocked?

Emergency sewer line repair in Syracuse, UT is answered 24/7. If sewage is backing up, stop using water and keep people away from the affected area while you wait. We will clear the line first when we can, then explain what the camera found.

The first question is simple: is the pipe damaged, or is something just stuck in it? A cleaned line that clogs again within weeks often has a crack, a sag or roots at a joint. A camera shows which, and repair only makes sense when it shows damage.

Sewage backup

What to do while a sewer backup is happening

  1. Stop running water: laundry, showers, dishwasher.
  2. Keep toilets unflushed until the line is cleared.
  3. Keep people and pets away from any room with sewage on the floor, and avoid outlets near the water.
  4. Call the 24/7 line. If neighbors are reporting the same backup, the North Davis Sewer District accepts reports on its lines.

Older pipe

Cast iron, clay and other older sewer pipe

Most of Syracuse was built after 1990, so cast iron and clay sewer pipe is not common here. Older farmhouses and homes from the town's early decades can still have it. Cast iron corrodes from the inside and may flake and crack, and clay joints let roots in.

A cracked cast iron section can sometimes be repaired with a sleeve or coupling. A badly scaled run is usually replaced. Roots in clay are cut out, and the pipe is lined or replaced afterward.

Local note

Dig safely, and call 811 first

Any sewer repair that digs in the yard needs utility lines marked first. In Utah that is done through Blue Stakes, and the North Davis Sewer District's emergency page points people to 811 for pipe locates. A plumber who calls before digging protects you from hitting a gas or power line.

For work on lines tied to the city system, ask Syracuse City Public Works what permits apply. Permit rules vary by job, so the city is the source for your address.

Pricing

What drives a sewer repair price

  • Length and depth of the damaged section.
  • Repair method: lining, bursting, spot repair or replacement.
  • Surface restoration: lawn, concrete or a driveway over the line.
  • Permits and inspections that apply to the work.
  • Whether the line also needs cleaning, jetting or a second camera run.

Insurance often does not cover a worn sewer line, so ask your insurer about sewer backup endorsements. We give you a clear price before work starts and explain how a bigger scope would change it.

Code note

Slope, cleanouts and the standard

Utah's state construction code amends the International Plumbing Code (IPC) for use statewide, under Utah Code Title 15A, Chapter 3, Part 3. Sewer lines depend on correct slope, bedding and cleanouts, and a repair that ignores them can fail again.

A replacement should include a cleanout where there was none. It makes every future service call faster.
