Sewage coming up: first moves while you wait

Emergency sewer line cleaning in Syracuse, UT means a plumber clears the main line so waste flows out of the house again. Our line is answered 24/7, nights and weekends included. These steps protect you while we are on the way.

  1. Stop all water use: no showers, laundry or dishwasher.
  2. Do not flush. Every flush adds water to a line that cannot take it.
  3. Move people and pets out of rooms with sewage, and stay away from outlets near it.
  4. Open windows if the odor is strong, and call the line.

Tell the dispatcher where the backup shows up, such as a basement floor drain or the lowest tub. That points to the main line, and it tells us to bring the cabling machine.

Causes

Why main lines back up

Main lines fail to drain for a few reasons. Roots work into joints and catch paper. Grease coats the pipe wall and narrows the opening. A belly or a cracked section holds water and solids. A foreign object, such as a wipe wad or a child's toy, lodges in the line.

We clear the blockage with a cabling machine first and use a camera to see what caused it. If grease is the issue, hydro jetting cleans the full wall. If the camera shows damage, the next step is repair, and we show you the footage.

Storm and yard drains

Yard drains, area drains and storm water

Not every outdoor drain is a sewer. Area drains in a yard, window well drains and driveway drains are on your property and can clog with silt, leaves and roots. We clear those too. Ask Syracuse City Public Works about storm drains in the street, since those are the city's to clean.

A yard drain that fills during a thunderstorm and then drains slowly is a classic silt problem, and cleaning it before the next storm saves a wet basement.

Local note

Your line, the District's line

Syracuse sewage flows to the North Davis Sewer District's plant, which sits in Syracuse near the Great Salt Lake shoreline. The District says to report sewage backups, backflow or overflowing manholes on lines it owns, and its emergency page lists after-hours numbers.

If only your house backs up, the blockage is almost certainly in your pipes, and a plumber is the right first call. If neighbors have the same trouble, report it to the District as well and call us for the inside lines.

Prevention

Keeping a main line clear afterward

  • Keep grease out of the sink. Wipe pans before washing and bin the grease.
  • Flush only toilet paper. Wipes marked flushable still catch in older lines.
  • Have roots cut and the pipe repaired if the camera shows an open joint.
  • Add a cleanout if the line has none, so future service is faster and cheaper.

A line that backs up twice in a year deserves a camera inspection, not another cable.

Pricing

What sets the price of cleaning a main line

  • Access: a cleanout outside is quick. A pulled toilet takes longer.
  • Line length and the distance to the blockage.
  • Whether roots, grease or a foreign object caused it.
  • A camera look before or after the cleaning.
  • After-hours service versus a scheduled visit.

Many homeowner policies treat sewer backup as an endorsement, so ask your insurer what yours says before you need it.
