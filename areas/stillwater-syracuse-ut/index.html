The area

What Stillwater is, and where to find it

Stillwater is listed in Syracuse City's General Plan among the approved planned residential developments. Real estate sites list the homes as Still Water, and the listed addresses run along Parkview Drive.

Parkview Drive addresses from roughly 1600 West to 2200 West appear in public listings. One record tagged Still Water shows a home at 1812 West Parkview Drive, built in 2018, with 1,758 square feet and a lot of about 0.09 acre.

Another Parkview Drive record is a numbered unit, which suggests multi-unit buildings sit among the single-family homes. The city does not publish a Stillwater boundary, so we confirm your address on the call.

Why it matters

How a small-lot home changes plumbing problems

  • Lines run close to a neighbor's wall, so a slow leak can reach two homes.
  • The main shutoff may sit in a tight garage corner or utility closet.
  • A second-floor bathroom puts a leak above finished ceilings.
  • A water heater in a closet leaves little room to work.
  • Association rules may decide who fixes what.

None of this is a fault in the neighborhood. It is simply how compact lots work, and it means a quick shutoff and a clear plan count for more than usual.

Stillwater local note

City water, irrigation and sewer basics

The state's Syracuse Water Element says the city pumps water to a three million gallon tower built in 2021, and mains feed homes by gravity from there. Shutoffs and pressure still vary house by house, so locate your own main valve today.

Outdoor watering runs from the city's secondary system between May 1 and October 1. Small lots have little yard, but a broken sprinkler line still floods a driveway or a neighbor's strip.

Sewage is handled by the North Davis Sewer District. In a multi-unit building, ask your association which section of line is shared before you pay for a repair.
