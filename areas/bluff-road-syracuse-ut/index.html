If water is flowing

Water or sewage showing up now? Start here

Whatever the age of the house, the first moves are the same. Water on the floor or a gurgling drain should not wait for a convenient day.

  1. Close the main water valve if a pipe is leaking.
  2. Stop using sinks, tubs and toilets if drains are backing up.
  3. Keep people and pets away from standing water and sewage.
  4. Photograph the damage, then call for help.
The area

The Bluff Road area on the map

The General Plan names the Bluff Road area at the corner of 2000 West, Bluff Road and 2700 South. The city has studied a roundabout there and plans to extend Bluff Road south toward Layton Parkway. A townhome project called Village at the Bluff is on the plan's approved list.

The road itself is a mix of vintages. One public listing for 1969 South Bluff Road shows a 2002 build on about 0.31 acre. Another, at 2455 South Bluff Road, shows a 1980 home with a 1998 remodel on about 0.48 acre.

Two records are not a survey, so we do not guess the age of your house. What they show is a road where older and newer homes sit side by side on lots large enough to matter for pipe runs.

Worth knowing

Age alone does not tell you what is in the ground

Some homes here were built decades apart, and remodels can swap out pipes without changing the address history. The only way to know what your sewer line is made of, and what shape it is in, is to look.

A sewer inspection puts a camera in the line and shows what is there. If it finds damage, sewer line repair is the next question. If it finds nothing, you have a baseline for later.

Larger lots

Why a longer run from the street adds plumbing questions

  • A longer water service line crosses more ground from the meter to the house.
  • A longer sewer lateral has more joints where roots or settling can cause trouble.
  • Trees on a larger lot can reach lines that stay clear on a small lot.
  • Outbuildings and yard fixtures may share the same supply.

Low pressure through the whole house, or a lawn strip that stays wet, can point at the buried water line. Read about water service line work if either sounds familiar.

Bluff Road local note

City water, sewer district and a neighbor city

Syracuse's water comes from a city well and Weber Basin water, per the state's Syracuse Water Element. Sewage goes to the North Davis Sewer District, whose treatment plant sits on the city's west border.

If the problem is only in your house, the blockage is on your side of the line. Layton borders Syracuse to the southeast, so homes near the city line should confirm which city they are in. Our Layton page covers that side.
