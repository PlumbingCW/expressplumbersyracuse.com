The area

Where Shoreline sits in Syracuse

Syracuse City's General Plan lists Shoreline among the planned residential communities approved in the city, next to Stillwater and The Fields. It is a west side name, and it is mostly used for newer single-family neighborhoods.

The city has not published a boundary map for it. Public listings place Shoreline Drive and Coastline Drive around 2900 to 3050 South, and builder communities that use the Shoreline name appear from roughly 2400 West to 2950 West. Treat those edges as approximate.

That matters when you call. Give us your street name and cross street, and we confirm the area instead of guessing from a neighborhood label.

Worth knowing

Newer home does not mean no plumbing questions

Public listing records for Shoreline Drive show homes built in 2023. The General Plan says most homes in Syracuse overall went up between 2000 and 2009, so this part of the city sits at the newer end.

Check your paperwork before you pay for a repair. A builder warranty or an appliance warranty may still cover the problem. Ask the builder or the manufacturer first, then call a plumber for anything outside that coverage.

Shoreline local note

Water, irrigation and sewer facts that shape a call

Syracuse drinks from a city well plus water bought from the Weber Basin Water Conservancy District, according to the state's Syracuse Water Element. Lawn and garden water comes from a separate pressurized secondary system that runs from May 1 to October 1.

That split helps you diagnose a wet yard. Close your home's main valve and watch the spot. If it keeps growing in the irrigation season, the secondary line is a likelier source than the indoor pipes.

State law requires secondary water metering by January 1, 2030, and the city reports its project is about 90 percent complete. Sewage goes to the North Davis Sewer District. A backup that hits only your house points to your own line.
