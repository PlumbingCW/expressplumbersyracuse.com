How to Detect Water Leaking in Your Syracuse, UT Home

A hidden leak wastes water, raises the bill and rots framing. This guide shows a meter test you can run in ten minutes and the signs that a leak is already working.

By the Syracuse Express Plumbing team. Published November 16, 2026

Short answer

Run the meter test first

The fastest way to learn if water is escaping is to read the water meter with everything off. Close every tap, stop the dishwasher and washer, and make sure no one flushes. Find the meter and look at the low-flow indicator, the small triangle or dial that spins at the slightest flow.

If it moves with every fixture off, water is leaking somewhere between the meter and your fixtures. If it holds still, you probably have no leak on the supply side.

Step by step

The ten minute test

Prepare

Turn off all water use inside and outside the house.

Read

Note the meter reading and the position of the low-flow indicator.

Wait

Leave everything off for fifteen minutes, then read again.

Compare

Any change means a leak. Shut the main in the house and read again to learn which side it is on.

If the meter keeps moving with the main shut, the leak is in the service line between the meter and the house. If it stops, the leak is inside the house.

Safety

Do not touch the city side

Syracuse City Code says the city owns the meter and the shutoff valve at it, and only city agents may adjust meters. Read the meter, but do not turn the city's valve. Use the main valve inside your house.

If the meter shows a leak and you cannot find it, call us. A plumber can pinpoint the break without digging up the yard.

Look around

Signs of a hidden leak

High bill

A sudden jump with the same habits.

Damp spots

Stains on a ceiling or wall, or a musty smell.

Sound

Running water when nothing is on.

Wet lawn

A soggy strip that does not dry, or an unusually green patch.

Low pressure

A drop at every fixture.

Warm floor

A warm spot on the floor can point to a hot water line.

Local note

Irrigation leaks and the secondary system

Syracuse yards run on pressurized secondary water from the Weber River, and the city plan lists a May to October season. A soggy strip in summer could be an irrigation leak and not a house leak. Shut the main in the house and read the culinary meter, and the answer tells you which system is leaking.

Syracuse City Code requires irrigation leaks to be repaired within 15 days of notice, so an irrigation leak is not one to leave. For a leak on the house side of the line, see water service line repair.

Quick tests

Two more checks you can run at home

Toilet leak test: add a few drops of food coloring to the tank, wait fifteen minutes without flushing and look at the bowl. Color in the bowl means the flapper leaks. A running toilet is the most common cause of a high water bill.

Faucet and hose check: look under every sink and at every hose bib for drips. A hose bib that drips only when a hose is attached can be leaking inside the wall.

After you find it

What to do once you know there is a leak

  1. Shut the main valve if water is actively escaping.
  2. Photograph the damage and note the meter reading and time.
  3. Dry the area and open windows or run a fan.
  4. Call a plumber to pinpoint and repair the leak.
  5. Tell your insurer promptly if there is damage.

Moisture left in a wall invites mold within days, so drying matters as much as the repair itself.
