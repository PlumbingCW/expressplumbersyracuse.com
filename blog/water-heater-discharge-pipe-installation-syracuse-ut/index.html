Water Heater Discharge Pipe Installation in Syracuse, UT

The relief valve discharge pipe is a small part with a big job. Here is what it does, how it should run and the mistakes that turn it into a hazard.

By the Syracuse Express Plumbing team. Published November 16, 2026

What it is

The relief valve and the pipe behind it

Every tank water heater has a temperature and pressure relief valve. If the heater gets too hot or the pressure climbs too high, the valve opens and lets water out, so the tank does not rupture. The discharge pipe carries that water to a safe place.

The pipe matters because the water can be scalding. A valve with no pipe sprays hot water at whoever stands nearby, and a badly routed pipe can freeze, block or drip where it does damage.

Do it right

How the discharge pipe should be installed

  • The pipe is at least the size of the valve outlet.
  • It runs downward the whole way, with no upward turns.
  • It has no valve, cap or plug anywhere on it.
  • It ends at a safe point, such as a floor drain or outdoors, in view.
  • It is made of an approved material for hot water.

A plugged or capped pipe is one of the most dangerous mistakes in a utility room. If you see one, call a plumber, and do not try to uncap it with the tank under pressure.

Common errors

Mistakes we see most

Capped or threaded end

Someone sealed a dripping valve instead of fixing it.

Pipe too short

It stops above the floor and sprays across the room.

Rising run

Water pools and the pipe does not drain.

Wrong material

A thin plastic that cannot take hot water.

Draining into a sink

The pipe ends where no one can see a discharge.

A valve that drips constantly is telling you something. Pressure may be high, or the valve may be worn. See water heater repair for what we check.

Local note

Ask what applies in Syracuse

Syracuse City Code does not adopt a plumbing code as a whole, so ask Syracuse City Public Works at 801.825.7235 which standards and inspections apply to your water heater job. A licensed plumber knows the manufacturer's rules and the adopted state standards, and can tell you what the city expects.

Frost matters too. A discharge pipe that ends outdoors can freeze shut in a Syracuse winter. If the plan is to run it outside, tell your plumber so the end is routed somewhere that stays clear.

Testing

How a plumber checks the valve

A plumber lifts the valve's test lever briefly and watches water flow out through the pipe and into the drain. The valve should reseat and stop dripping when released. If it keeps dripping, the seat is worn or debris is stuck, and the valve is replaced.

Do not test the valve yourself while standing near the pipe. The water can be scalding, and a valve that does not reseat can be hard to stop. Leave the test to a plumber who can shut the heater off first.

Warning signs

When to call right away

  • Steam or hissing from the valve or the pipe.
  • A rumbling or popping tank.
  • A discharge pipe that is hot to the touch.
  • Water pooling under the pipe or the heater.

Shut off the gas or power to the heater and call. A relief valve that is discharging means the tank is too hot, too high in pressure or both.

Materials

Which materials are suitable

The pipe must handle hot water and the temperature a relief event can reach. Copper, CPVC and some rated plastics are used, and the right choice depends on the installation rules and the manufacturer's instructions. Do not use thin garden hose, vinyl tubing or thin plastic pipe, which can soften or burst.

One more thing

Keep the area around the heater clear

Do not store anything where the discharge pipe ends, and do not stack boxes against the tank or its vent. A clear floor lets you see a drip early, which is the cheapest warning you will get.

Standard

What the code says about the relief valve pipe

Utah's state construction code amends the International Plumbing Code (IPC) for use statewide, under Utah Code Title 15A, Chapter 3, Part 3. The IPC governs how a relief valve discharge pipe is sized and routed, and it forbids valves or caps in the line.
