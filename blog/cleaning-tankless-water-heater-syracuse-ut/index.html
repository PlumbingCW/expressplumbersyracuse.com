How to Clean a Tankless Water Heater in Syracuse, UT

Scale slowly chokes a tankless heater. A yearly flush keeps it efficient, and knowing when to stop and call a plumber keeps you safe.

By the Syracuse Express Plumbing team. Published November 16, 2026

Start here

Why a tankless heater needs cleaning

A tankless heater heats water as it flows through a small heat exchanger. Minerals in the water bake onto that exchanger over time, and the scale narrows the passages and insulates the metal. The unit works harder, heats less evenly and may show an error code or shut down.

A yearly flush removes most of that scale. The job takes about an hour and a half, and it does not need special skill, but it does need the right fittings and some care.

Gather first

What you need for a flush

  • A small submersible pump, two hoses and a five-gallon bucket.
  • Two to three gallons of white vinegar, or a descaling solution the manufacturer approves.
  • A flush kit or isolation valves, which let you hook up the pump.
  • The unit's manual, since steps and approved solutions differ.

If your heater lacks isolation valves, a plumber can add them, and that is worth doing. See tankless water heater service for what that visit looks like.

Step by step

The flush, in order

Power down

Shut off the gas and electricity, then close the water valves.

Connect

Attach hoses to the service valves, one to the pump and one back to the bucket.

Circulate

Pour vinegar into the bucket and run the pump for about 45 minutes.

Rinse

Drain the vinegar, flush with clean water and clear the inlet screen, then restore power.

Run hot water at a faucet afterward and listen. Any hissing, leaking at the hoses or an error code that stays means something is not right, and you should stop and call.

Know the limits

When to stop and call a plumber

You can handle

  • A yearly flush on a unit with isolation valves.
  • Cleaning the inlet screen.
  • Reading the manual.

Call a plumber

  • Gas smell or any sign of exhaust problems.
  • Leaks from the unit or the vent.
  • Repeated error codes after a flush.

A gas smell is not a DIY problem. Leave the building, call 911 and Enbridge Gas at 1-800-767-1689, and call us once the area is safe.

Local note

What Syracuse water means for scale

Syracuse's culinary water comes from city Well #3 and water purchased from Weber Basin. The state water documents we found do not give a hardness figure, so we cannot tell you how fast scale builds in your unit. The practical answer is to test your tap and ask Syracuse City Public Works at 801.825.7235 what the supply looks like.

If you see white crust on faucets and showerheads, scale is forming, and a yearly flush is the minimum. A water softener is worth a look in that case, because it protects the heater and the fixtures together.

Worth knowing

Signs you waited too long

  • Hot water that fluctuates between hot and lukewarm.
  • A unit that clicks off during a shower.
  • An error code tied to flow or temperature.
  • Noisy operation with a rumbling or popping sound.
  • Longer wait for hot water than before.

If the unit has these signs and a flush does not cure them, see water heater repair. A scaled heat exchanger can need professional cleaning or replacement.

Maintenance plan

A simple yearly routine

  1. Schedule the flush for the same month each year so you do not forget.
  2. Clean the inlet screen at the same time. Debris on the screen cuts flow.
  3. Check the vent and the air intake for dust and blockage.
  4. Write the date and what you saw on a label on the heater.
  5. If the unit is more than a few years old, ask a plumber to look it over once.

A written log is useful when a repair visit comes up. It shows the plumber what changed and when, and it helps with a warranty claim.

Quick answers

Common mistakes during a flush

  • Forgetting to close the gas before connecting hoses.
  • Using a descaler the manufacturer does not approve.
  • Skipping the inlet screen, which then clogs again within weeks.
  • Reopening the valves slowly, which can trap air in the lines.
  • Ignoring an error code that returns after the flush.

If you make one of these mistakes and something looks wrong, stop and call before running the unit again.
