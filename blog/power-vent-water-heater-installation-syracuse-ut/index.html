Power Vent Water Heater Installation in Syracuse, UT

A power vent heater solves the problem of a short or awkward flue run, and it asks more of the installer than a standard tank. Here is what the job involves.

By the Syracuse Express Plumbing team. Published November 16, 2026

The short answer

What a power vent water heater is

A power vent water heater is a gas tank unit with a fan that pushes exhaust out through a plastic vent pipe. A standard atmospheric heater relies on a hot flue to rise through a metal vent to the roof. The fan frees the installer to run the vent through a sidewall, which helps when the roof route is long, blocked or not allowed.

That flexibility costs you something. The fan needs a 120-volt outlet, the unit makes some fan noise and a failed fan or pressure switch takes the heater down. If your utility room has no outlet nearby, the job grows by an electrical step.

Is it right for you

Four reasons homeowners choose power vent

No roof route

A finished ceiling or a long run to the roof makes a sidewall vent simpler.

Tight utility room

Some power vent units pull combustion air from the room or from outside, which matters in a closed closet.

Replacing a tank in a new layout

A moved heater location can still reach a sidewall.

Efficiency goals

Power vent units can run more efficiently than atmospheric tanks, and ratings vary by model.

If none of those fits your house, a standard tank may be the simpler choice. Our water heater installation page explains how we choose.

What the install needs

The parts of a power vent installation

Gas line

Correct size and a shutoff within reach, with a drip leg and a leak test.

Vent run

Plastic vent pipe with the slope, support and termination distances the manufacturer requires.

Power

A 120-volt outlet or a hardwired connection for the fan and controls.

Water side

Shutoffs, flex lines, an expansion tank where needed and a relief valve with a discharge pipe.

Each part has a failure point. A badly sloped vent collects condensate, a wrong gas size starves the burner and a missing discharge pipe turns a relief event into a flood.

The power outage row matters in winter. A gas tank with a standing pilot keeps heating when the grid is down. A power vent unit stops with the fan. If hot water during an outage matters to you, say so when you plan.

Local note

Check the license and ask about the permit

Gas water heater work belongs with a licensed plumber. The Utah Division of Occupational and Professional Licensing has a Verify a License tool at secure.utah.gov/llv/search/index.html, and plumbing license types include apprentice, journeyman, residential journeyman, master and residential master. Check the name on the license before anyone starts.

Syracuse City Code does not adopt a plumbing code as a whole, so ask Syracuse City Public Works at 801.825.7235 which permits and inspections apply to your address. If a gas smell ever appears, leave the building, then call 911 and Enbridge Gas at 1-800-767-1689 first.

If your old heater has failed and you want a power vent unit, the job is a replacement with extra venting and power steps. See water heater replacement for what that visit covers. For a new location, the same steps apply, plus a drain and sometimes a new gas line.

Before the install day

Prepare the utility room

  • Clear a three-foot path to the heater and the work area.
  • Know where the main water and gas shutoffs are.
  • Plan for water to be off for part of the day.
  • Move stored items away from the old heater.

A clear room speeds the job and keeps the work safe around gas and hot water.

Maintenance

Care for a power vent unit after it is in

Power vent heaters need the same yearly attention as any tank, plus a look at the fan and the vent. Listen for a change in fan noise, check the vent pipe for sagging or loose joints and make sure the air intake is clear of dust and lint.

Flush a few gallons from the tank drain each year to clear sediment, and test the relief valve with a plumber's guidance. A heater with a logged service history is easier to diagnose and easier to sell with the house.

Code note

Vent clearances are not optional

Utah's state construction code amends the International Plumbing Code (IPC) for use statewide, under Utah Code Title 15A, Chapter 3, Part 3. Power vent terminations have required distances from windows, doors and air intakes, and the manufacturer's manual adds its own.

A plumber who measures those clearances before drilling the wall saves you from moving a vent later.

Safety steps

What to do if something smells wrong after the install

  1. If you smell gas, leave the building, then call 911 and Enbridge Gas at 1-800-767-1689 first. Do not use switches or phones inside.
  2. If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, get everyone outside into fresh air and call 911.
  3. If the fan stops or the vent pipe separates, shut off the heater and call the plumber who installed it.
  4. When to call us: any vent noise, soot, or a heater that shuts off repeatedly.
