Installing a New Toilet Flange in Syracuse, UT Homes

A toilet that rocks, leaks at the base or smells of sewer gas may have a failed flange. Here is how to tell, and what the repair involves.

By the Syracuse Express Plumbing team. Published November 16, 2026

Basics

What a toilet flange does

The flange is the ring that anchors a toilet to the floor and connects it to the drain. A wax ring or a rubber gasket seals the joint between the toilet and the flange. When the flange cracks, rusts or sits too low, the seal fails and the toilet rocks or leaks.

A leak at the base looks small, but the water soaks the subfloor and can rot it over time. The earlier you fix it, the less you repair afterward.

Diagnosis

Signs the flange has failed

Rocking

The toilet moves when you sit, even after tightening the bolts.

Water at the base

A ring of water after a flush, with the supply line and tank dry.

Sewer smell

A broken seal lets gas escape into the room.

Soft floor

Spongy flooring around the base points to a long-running leak.

A loose supply line or tank sweat can mimic a base leak, so dry the base and watch where the water first appears. If the leak is real, see toilet repair for what the visit covers.

Process

How a flange repair goes

Pull the toilet

Shut the water, flush, drain the tank and lift the toilet off.

Inspect

Check the flange for cracks, rust and height relative to the finished floor.

Repair

Use a repair ring or replace the flange if it is damaged. Set it at the right height.

Reset

Fit a new wax ring or gasket, set the toilet and tighten the bolts evenly.

The flange should sit on top of the finished floor, not below it. A flange set too low leaves a gap the wax cannot bridge.

Local note

Where the toilet drains in Syracuse

Syracuse toilets drain to the North Davis Sewer District, whose treatment plant sits in Syracuse near the Great Salt Lake shoreline. A failed flange is a problem on your side of the line. If every toilet in the house backs up together, the blockage is in the main line, not the flange, and that is a sewer line cleaning job.

The District's daytime line is 801.825.0712, and it asks you to report overflowing manholes and backups on its own lines. For a leak at the toilet base, you need a plumber, not the District.

Do it right

When a new flange also means a new toilet

If the toilet itself is cracked or the finish is gone, replace it while the flange is exposed. Pulling a toilet twice is waste. See plumbing installation for fixtures and fittings.

If you plan a remodel, mention it when you call. Raising the floor height changes what the flange needs, and we would rather scope it once.

Parts list

What a flange repair uses

  • A new wax ring or a wax-free gasket.
  • New closet bolts and washers.
  • A flange repair ring, if the flange is cracked but the pipe is sound.
  • A spacer or extension ring when the new floor sits higher.

Do not reuse an old wax ring. Once compressed it will not seal again, and a reused ring is a common reason for a second leak.

Time and tools

What the visit takes

Most flange repairs take one visit. The plumber shuts the water, pulls the toilet, checks the flange and the subfloor, makes the repair and resets the toilet with a new seal. A wet or soft subfloor adds time, because it has to be dried or repaired first.

Tell us on the call if the toilet has been leaking for a long while. We will bring materials for a floor repair, and we will explain what we find before we start.

Standard

Flange height and the code

Utah's state construction code amends the International Plumbing Code (IPC) for use statewide, under Utah Code Title 15A, Chapter 3, Part 3. It expects the flange to sit on the finished floor and to be anchored to it, which is why a low flange after a floor upgrade is a fix and not a preference.
