Overview

Tankless water heater service in Syracuse, UT

A tankless water heater heats water as it flows, so it has no tank to leak or run empty. That convenience comes with its own needs: a correctly sized gas line or electric supply, a proper vent, and a regular flush to clear scale from the heat exchanger.

We install tankless units, diagnose error codes and repair faults, and service them on a schedule. If your tank has failed and you want to switch, the replacement page covers the swap.

Flush and descale

Why a tankless heater needs a yearly flush

Minerals in water settle as scale on the inside of a tankless heat exchanger. Scale cuts flow, makes the unit work harder and triggers error codes. A flush circulates a descaling solution, usually white vinegar or a manufacturer-approved product, through the unit and then rinses it clean.

  • Service valves must be installed so the unit can be isolated and flushed.
  • Run time is commonly about an hour, then a rinse and a check of the inlet screen.
  • Units that were never flushed need more careful handling, so call before you try it yourself.
  • The right flush interval depends on your water, which is why a tap test is the starting point.

We handle the flush and check the inlet filter and the vent at the same visit.

Right unit, right house

What a tankless install needs

A whole-home gas tankless unit often needs a larger gas line than a tank heater, a vent material approved for the unit, and a condensate drain on high-efficiency models. Electric tankless units need substantial electrical capacity, so the panel and wire run decide whether the unit is practical.

Local note

Test the tap before you pick a flush schedule

Syracuse's culinary water comes from a city well and from Weber Basin Water Conservancy District supply, according to the city's water plan on file with the state. That plan gives no hardness number, and we will not guess one for your street.

So the sensible schedule starts with your own tap. A hardness test kit costs little, and Syracuse City Public Works is the place to ask for the water quality report. Harder water means flushing more often, softer water less often.

Code note

Gas size, venting and the standard they follow

Utah's state construction code amends the International Plumbing Code (IPC) for use statewide, under Utah Code Title 15A, Chapter 3, Part 3. Tankless units draw a large burst of gas, so the gas line size and the vent material must match the maker's table and the code.

A plumber who sizes the line from the unit's input rating, and not from what is already there, protects both the warranty and your safety.
